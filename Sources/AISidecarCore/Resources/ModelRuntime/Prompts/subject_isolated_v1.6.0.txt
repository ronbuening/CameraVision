PROMPT_VERSION: aisidecar.prompt.subject_isolated/1.6.0

Analyze the isolated subject crop and return exactly one JSON object matching the supplied JSON Schema.

The background has been removed, replaced, cropped away, or composited onto a neutral matte.
Judge only the subject itself: morphology, shape, structure, color pattern, field marks, pose, object type, and object parts.
Do not infer habitat, location, weather, season, time of day, event, or scene context.
Do not describe the matte or the missing background as a real object, surface, or setting.
The response must not contain scene_context or habitat_or_setting fields.

OUTPUT RULES

Return only the JSON object: no Markdown, no code fences, no comments, no fields beyond the schema.
Include every required field in every response, including species.
Use [] for any array field with nothing to report, except genre_or_photography_type, which needs at least one best-fit entry.
Use "" for uncertainty_notes when there is nothing useful to note.
Confidence is only high, medium, or low:
- high: directly visible and unlikely to be wrong.
- medium: plausible from visible evidence, but not fully certain.
- low: weak visual support; include only when still useful for review.

EVIDENCE RULES

Base every term on visible subject evidence, not on what is merely likely.
Prefer a broader correct term over a narrower guess.
Evidence strings are short visual descriptions, not reasoning.
Good evidence: "yellow bill and white body", "curved metal handle", "fine hairs on stem".
Bad evidence: "common in Florida", "likely because of the background", "probably photographed in a garden".
Do not propose camera, lens, editing, software, file-format, image-quality, or text-transcription terms.
Use concise English terms suitable for later metadata normalization.

GENRE RULES

genre_or_photography_type: 1 to 4 entries, most likely first, chosen only from:
architecture, landscape, seascape, cityscape, portrait_people, group_portrait, candid_people, street_documentary, wildlife, bird_photography, plant_botanical, macro_closeup, abstract, still_life, product, food, sports_action, event, travel, night, astrophotography, aerial, underwater, interior, fine_art_conceptual.
Be conservative with genres that depend on missing background context; if no strong genre is clear, use the best broad match with low confidence.
Genre evidence must come from the visible subject, never from the removed background.

SPECIES RULES

species is always present.
When no genre entry is wildlife, bird_photography, or plant_botanical, species must be [].
Otherwise list biological identity candidates supported by visible field marks on the subject, using species-level common names when the visible evidence supports them.
Broader taxa such as heron, egret, hawk, oak, or wildflower are correct when narrower identification is uncertain.
Never derive a species from habitat, season, commonness, likelihood, or the removed background; use [] when nothing is visually defensible.
Keep exact species and taxonomy terms in species only, not in proposed_keywords; note weak or competing identifications in uncertainty_notes.

FIELD GUIDE

summary: one concise sentence about the isolated subject; mention the crop or matte only when it affects uncertainty.
main_subjects: the dominant isolated subject or subjects; specific terms when supported, broader terms when identifying traits are missing; describe people without identity claims.
secondary_subjects: meaningful secondary subjects or subject parts that support identification, such as wing, bill, flower petals, leaf, label, handle, or wheel; ignore mask-edge artifacts unless they affect uncertainty.
behavior_or_action: visible pose, motion, gesture, or object state supported by the subject alone.
proposed_keywords: concise metadata terms for subject traits, body or object parts, color patterns, behavior, and genre; no exact species/taxonomy duplicates, no habitat/setting/location/event terms unless visible on the subject itself, no private identity or sensitive attributes, no text transcription.
uncertainty_notes: brief notes on partial visibility, crop truncation, mask-edge ambiguity, occlusion, or competing identifications; "" when none.

QUALITY ASSESSMENT

The supplied schema includes quality_assessment. Complete every required field in that object as a separate evaluation of the isolated subject.

Keep quality separate from tagging. The base prompt's ban on image-quality terms applies to genre_or_photography_type, species, main_subjects, secondary_subjects, behavior_or_action, proposed_keywords, and candidate evidence. Put quality judgments only in quality_assessment.

Use only visible subject evidence for quality. Do not use GPS, EXIF, filename, location, species likelihood, or other external context. Do not estimate numeric sharpness, clipping, noise, color accuracy, resolution, capture settings, or edit history. Rate focus, fine detail, and artifacts only when visible at the supplied crop resolution; otherwise use unrated.

QUALITY LEVELS

- problem: a clear visible weakness that reduces subject quality.
- acceptable: adequate or neutral; not a notable strength.
- strong: clearly supports the rendering or presentation of the subject.
- unrated: not relevant or not reliably judgeable.

Do not use strong merely because no problem is visible. Assess only the isolated subject as shown. Do not assess whole-frame composition, framing, background, habitat, setting, or subject-background relationship, and do not infer the original frame.

Ignore the neutral matte, removed background, crop boundary, and ordinary isolation edges. Treat an isolation artifact as a concern only when it obscures or alters subject detail. When missing or cropped detail prevents a reliable judgment, use unrated rather than problem.

Judge intentional choices by their visible effect. Do not mark blur, darkness, grain, shallow depth of field, or unusual color as problems merely because they are unconventional. Use unrated for pose_expression_or_moment when pose, gesture, behavior, or expression is not relevant. technical_cleanliness covers only obvious visible defects on the subject.

overall_subject_quality is a separate holistic judgment of the subject only, not an average.
strengths and concerns contain at most two short visible observations each, must agree with the ratings, and use [] when none are clear. Do not give editing instructions.
confidence is confidence in the assessment, not a quality score.

Return only the JSON object.
